Hot water in an Algarve holiday home: heat pumps, seasonal use and legionella

A holiday home sits empty for months and then fills up all at once: the family in August, friends at Easter, guests during a heatwave. The hot water system has to cope with that peak, use no energy while the house is empty and deliver safe water for the first shower. Choosing and setting up the equipment for this is different from doing it for a home lived in all year, especially when the owner lives abroad.

Published: 13 Nov 2026 · Reading time: 8 min · Author: iStarTec

What hot water system works best in a holiday home?

In most holiday homes, a heat pump water heater with scheduling and remote control, sized for a full house rather than the yearly average. It stays in holiday mode while the house is closed and heats up again before you arrive, with a cycle at 60 °C or above. If the house is used mainly in summer, solar thermal with the heat pump as back-up makes the most of the sun in the weeks with the most people. iStarTec installs and sets up these systems with Bosch equipment.

  • Equipment: heat pump water heater with time scheduling and, ideally, app control
  • Size: for maximum occupancy: allow 40 to 50 litres per person per day
  • House closed: holiday mode; the tank does not need to be kept hot
  • Before arrival: heating cycle at 60 °C or above, then run water at every outlet
  • Solar thermal: very effective in a house used in summer, but needs overheating protection when it stands empty
  • Quote: free, after a technical visit, with a reply within 24 working hours

What changes in a house that stands empty

In a home lived in all year, the water in the tank and the pipes is renewed every day. In a holiday home it can stand for weeks or months. That brings three problems that barely exist in a home used daily:

  • Energy used for nothing. A tank kept hot in an empty house loses heat every day and heats up again, with nobody showering.
  • Standing water. Warm, still water in the tank, the pipes and the shower is where legionella multiplies.
  • Demand peaks. When the house fills up, everyone showers at the same time. A tank sized for two people will not cope with eight.

A heat pump water heater handles all three well if it is chosen and set up properly. It is a hot water tank with a compressor that takes heat from the air, with time scheduling, holiday mode and an anti-legionella cycle. With the Algarve's mild winters, it runs efficiently all year.

Standing water and legionella

The World Health Organization says legionella lives and multiplies in water between 20 and 50 °C, and recommends that hot water is stored and leaves the water heater at 60 °C or above. Infection happens by breathing in droplets of contaminated water, so the most sensitive point in a house is the shower.

In a holiday home this comes down to three practical rules:

  1. Before arrival, the tank runs a cycle at 60 °C or above. Current heat pumps have an anti-legionella programme that uses the compressor and the back-up element to reach that temperature. We leave it scheduled at start-up.
  2. On arrival, run hot and cold water at every outlet for a few minutes, starting with the showers. The water that stood in the pipes did not go through the tank cycle.
  3. Avoid outlets that are never used. An outdoor shower or a closed guest bathroom holds standing water. If they are not needed, the plumbing can be changed so it leaves no dead legs.

In a holiday rental, guests do not know how long the house was closed. The arrival routine has to be done by whoever prepares the house. If a management company looks after it, agree this step with them.

Do not switch off the anti-legionella cycle to save energy

It uses a little more energy on the days it runs. It is also what makes the water safe after weeks of standing. In a holiday home, it is the last function you should turn off.

Sizing for a full house

As a guide, allow 40 to 50 litres of hot water per person per day. In a holiday home, do the sum with the maximum number of people the house takes, not the yearly average.

5 to 6 people250 to 300-litre heat pumpWith two bathrooms in use at the same time, go for 300 litres
7 people or more300 litres with solar thermal, or two unitsVillas with a pool and many guests in summer

A starting point for the conversation on the technical visit. The final size depends on how many showers are taken at the same time and whether there is a bath.

A heat pump takes a few hours to reheat the whole tank. In a full house, what counts is the stored water, not the compressor's output. The back-up element helps on peak days, but if it runs every day, the tank is too small.

Controlling hot water from abroad

Owners who live outside the Algarve, or outside Portugal, gain a lot from equipment they can control remotely. Many current models have Wi-Fi and an app to:

  • switch holiday mode on and off;
  • schedule your return, so the tank heats up and runs the anti-legionella cycle the day before you arrive;
  • see the water temperature and error alerts without visiting the house;
  • match the schedule to an off-peak tariff, or to sunny hours if the house has solar panels.

For this to work, the house needs Wi-Fi left on while it is empty, with signal where the unit sits. We check this on the visit.

What about solar thermal in a holiday home?

An Algarve holiday home is usually full in exactly the sunniest months. In those months a solar thermal system heats almost all the water the house needs, and the heat pump stays as back-up for cloudy days and for spring and autumn.

The care is needed in the weeks when the house is empty in summer. With no hot water being used, the panel keeps sitting in the sun and the circuit overheats, which wears out the solar fluid faster. Current systems have a holiday mode on the controller and other protections, and it is why the primary circuit we install is always in copper. If the house is empty for much of the summer, a heat pump on its own is usually simpler.

Pipework, recirculation and standing water

In large villas, the furthest bathroom can be tens of metres from the tank. Without hot water recirculation, every shower starts with cold water running. With recirculation the water circulates and arrives hot quickly, but the circulation pump should only run during the hours of use, on a timer. Running 24 hours a day, it wastes energy and keeps a long pipe lukewarm.

If the plumbing is old, it is worth looking at it on the same visit: old pipework with dead legs holds standing water whatever is done at the tank. We explain this in our guide to replumbing an old house in Portugal.

The arrival and departure routine

When you leave

  • Put the unit in holiday mode, or schedule your return date.
  • Switch off hot water recirculation, if the house has it.
  • Do not unplug the unit if you want to control it remotely.

The day before you arrive

  • The tank heats up and runs the cycle at 60 °C or above (automatically, if it is scheduled).

When you arrive

  • Run hot and cold water at every shower and tap for a few minutes, without breathing the steam up close.
  • Switch recirculation back on.
  • Check on the display or the app that the temperature is normal.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I leave the heat pump on while the house is empty?

Plugged in, yes, but in holiday mode. There is no need to keep the tank hot in an empty house. Schedule your return so the tank heats up and runs the anti-legionella cycle before you arrive.

How far ahead should the heat pump switch on before I arrive?

Schedule it for the day before. A 200 to 300-litre tank can need several hours to heat up from cold, and the anti-legionella cycle adds a little more time.

Can I control the heat pump from my phone?

Many current models have Wi-Fi and an app to change mode, schedule your return and see alerts. The house needs Wi-Fi left on while it is empty. We confirm the model and the signal on the technical visit.

What size tank for a holiday rental with 8 guests?

At 40 to 50 litres per person per day, 8 guests need a lot more than a standard tank. The usual solution is a 300-litre tank with solar thermal, or two units. We decide on the visit, based on the number of bathrooms and when people shower.

How do I avoid legionella in a house that has been closed?

Have the tank run a cycle at 60 °C or above before you arrive, then run hot and cold water at every outlet for a few minutes, starting with the showers. The World Health Organization says legionella multiplies between 20 and 50 °C and recommends storing hot water at 60 °C or above.
