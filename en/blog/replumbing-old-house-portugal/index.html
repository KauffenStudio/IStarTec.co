Replumbing an old house: warning signs, materials and doing the work while you live there

A leak can be repaired. An old system has to be replaced. This guide helps you work out which situation your home is in, what is usually inside the walls of Algarve villas and apartments, and how a complete replacement goes without you having to move out.

Published: 13 Oct 2026 · Reading time: 10 min · Author: iStarTec

When is it worth replacing all the plumbing?

When leaks keep appearing in different places, the water runs brown after standing for hours, the pressure has dropped for no obvious reason, or the pipes are galvanised steel or lead. In those cases each repair fixes one spot and leaves the rest of the system to keep ageing. A replumb replaces the hot and cold water pipework with new pipe and manifolds and, where it makes sense, the internal drainage as well. It is done in stages, with the house occupied.

  • Warning signs: repeated leaks, cloudy or rusty water, low pressure, water hammer and noisy pipes
  • Always replace: lead and galvanised steel pipes
  • New system: multilayer pipe with manifolds; drainage in PP or PVC (Uponor, Geberit, Wavin)
  • Licence: as a rule not needed for interior works that do not touch the structure (RJUE, article 6)
  • Duration: 7 to 10 days in an apartment, 2 to 3 weeks in a three-bedroom (T3) villa
  • Quote: free, after a technical visit, with a reply within 24 working hours

Repair or replace

A single leak on a pipe that is otherwise in good condition can be repaired, and that is the end of it. The trouble starts when the leak is a symptom. In a galvanised steel system, corrosion eats away at the inside of every pipe at the same time: after the first leak come others, in different places, and each one can ruin a ceiling, a floor or the flat of the neighbour below.

These are the signs that lead us to recommend a full replacement, and what each one usually means:

SignWhat is usually going on
Brown or yellowish water after hours without useCorrosion inside galvanised steel pipes
Low pressure at several points in the houseLimescale and rust narrowing the inside of the pipes
Leaks in different places within a few yearsThe system has reached the end of its life; this is not a one-off fault
Banging or whistling when taps are turned offBadly secured pipes, wrong pipe sizes or excessive pressure
Hot water takes a long time to arriveLong pipe runs in series, with no insulation
Damp patches on walls or ceilingsA hidden leak, often at an old joint

If you recognise two or more of these, every new repair is money spent while the underlying problem stays where it is.

What is usually inside the walls

In houses built up to the 1990s we almost always find one of these materials. Often we find several, the result of repairs made over the years.

Lead pipes

EU Directive 2020/2184, transposed into Portuguese law by Decree-Law no. 69/2023, sets a limit of 10 micrograms of lead per litre of water at the tap and requires it to come down to 5 micrograms by 12 January 2036. With lead pipes inside the house, that limit is hard to guarantee, especially for the first water of the morning, which has spent the night standing in the pipes.

How to recognise a lead pipe: it is dull grey and soft, and if you scrape it with a key it turns shiny and silver. A magnet will not stick to it (on galvanised steel, it does). The joints often have a rounded, bulb-like look. We find lead mostly in old houses in historic centres, such as Tavira's.

Limescale and borehole water

Algarve water is hard. Limescale clings to the walls of old pipes and, together with rust, squeezes the bore until the pressure drops. In houses supplied by a borehole (furo), the water can carry sand or be more aggressive towards metals. On the visit we always ask where the water comes from, because that affects the choice of material and the protection where the supply enters the house, such as a filter or a pressure-reducing valve.

What the new system is made of

Almost all water systems are now built in one of these four materials. All of them are good when properly installed. The difference is in how well they adapt to a house that already exists and that people will be living in during the work.

MaterialHow it is joinedStrengthsLimitations
Multilayer (PEX-AL-PEX)Press fittingsBends and holds its shape; expands little; follows the routes of the old pipesNeeds quality fittings and the right press tool
PEXPress or compression fittingsVery flexible; good for awkward runsDoes not hold its shape; needs more fixings
PPRFusion welding (termofusão)Tough and widely used in new buildsRigid; expands a lot with hot water; the welds need room to work
CopperSoldered joints or press fittingsCopes with high temperatures; lasts for decadesMore labour; sensitive to aggressive water

In an occupied home our default choice is multilayer pipe with manifolds, from brands such as Uponor. We use PEX on awkward runs and copper where the temperature calls for it, as in solar panel circuits. For internal drainage we use branded PP or PVC (Geberit, Wavin). We compared the four materials in detail in our guide multilayer, PEX, PPR or copper (in Portuguese).

Manifolds instead of pipes in series

In old houses the water runs in series: the same pipe goes on from tap to tap. In the new system each outlet has its own pipe, running from a manifold. A leak then affects a single outlet, which you can shut off at the manifold without cutting the water to the whole house. And the shower pressure no longer drops when someone starts the washing machine.

Decisions to make before the work starts

With the walls open, adding or moving an outlet is simple. Once the tiles are on, it is not. These are the questions worth answering before you start:

  • Where will the manifold go? It has to stay accessible, in a cupboard or behind an access panel, close to the bathrooms and the kitchen.
  • Do you need new outlets? A kitchen island, a dishwasher, a second bathroom, a tap on the terrace.
  • Are you changing how you heat your water? If you are thinking of replacing the gas water heater with a heat pump or installing solar thermal, we leave the pipework ready for the tank's new location.
  • Are the bathrooms a long way from the hot water? In large villas, hot water recirculation (in Portuguese) saves the wait at every shower.
  • Do you want any pre-installations? A condensate drain for air conditioning or a central vacuum system are much easier with the walls open.
  • What about the drains? If the waste pipes are old (cast iron, fibre cement or thin-walled PVC), this is the right time to replace them. Fibre cement pipes may contain asbestos; removing them is subject to its own rules and is done by an authorised specialist company.

Apartment or villa: where your pipework ends

In an apartment building, your home's plumbing starts at the meter. The risers that run up through the building and the soil stacks are common parts, belonging to the condominium. We can replace everything inside your flat (the fração), but any work on the risers, or a shutdown of the building's water supply, has to be agreed with the administration. It is also worth warning the neighbour below before any chases are cut in the floor.

In a villa, the internal system runs from the meter to every outlet. The connection to the public mains is the responsibility of the municipal water utility (entidade gestora).

Do you need a licence?

In most cases, no. Article 6 of the Legal Regime for Urbanisation and Building (RJUE, Regime Jurídico da Urbanização e Edificação) exempts conservation works, and alterations inside buildings that do not touch the structure, the façades or the roof, from licensing. Replacing the plumbing along the same routes falls within this. In listed buildings or in protected areas of historic centres, check with the town hall (câmara municipal) first.

How the work goes while you live there

Replacing the plumbing is one of the most invasive jobs we do, alongside underfloor heating. With a clear method, the house keeps working:

  1. Technical visit. We identify the materials, the pipe routes and what needs replacing.
  2. Quote, detailed room by room, with a diagram of the new system.
  3. Work in stages. We install the new system while the old one keeps supplying the house.
  4. One planned water shutdown, agreed with you, for the final connections. There is always a working bathroom before and after.
  5. Pressure test at 10 bar for 24 hours, before the walls are closed.
  6. Photographs of every pipe route before closing up. You will want them the day you hang a cupboard or drill into a wall.
  7. Chases filled, ready for the builder or tiler to do the finishes.

We reuse the routes of the old pipework wherever possible, to cut as few chases as we can, and we seal off the work area to keep dust under control. In a three-bedroom villa the work usually takes 2 to 3 weeks. In an apartment, 7 to 10 days.

What we don't do

To save everyone time: we do not do one-off repairs or drain unblocking. If you have a single leak in a system that is in good condition, a repair plumber will sort it out faster. We also do not do tiling, painting or finishing masonry; we leave the chases filled and ready for whoever does that part.

We come in when the decision is to replace the whole system, or at least a full floor or a complete area of the house. That is where doing the job once works out better than ten repairs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do all the walls have to be broken open?

No. Wherever possible we reuse the routes of the old pipework and, on some runs, take the new pipes through false ceilings, service shafts (courettes) or skirting ducts. Some chasing is almost always unavoidable, especially in the bathrooms and the kitchen, where the outlets are in the wall.

Can I keep living in the house during the work?

Yes. We work in stages and the water is only off on the day of the final connections, agreed with you in advance. There is always a working bathroom before and after that shutdown.

How do I know if I have lead pipes?

Lead is dull grey and soft: scraped with a key it turns shiny and silver, and a magnet will not stick to it. It turns up mostly in old buildings. If you are not sure, we check on the technical visit. If it is lead, we always recommend replacing it, because it can release lead into the water.

Do I need a licence from the town hall to replace the plumbing?

As a rule, no. Article 6 of the RJUE (Portugal's Legal Regime for Urbanisation and Building) exempts conservation works and interior alterations that do not touch the structure, the façades or the roof. In listed buildings or protected areas, check with the câmara municipal (town hall). In an apartment building, any work on the common parts needs the condominium's agreement.

Is it worth replacing the drains at the same time?

If the walls and floors are being opened up and the drains are old, almost always yes. Doing both systems in one job means you do not have to break into the house again a few years later. We assess the condition of the drains on the technical visit.

How long does the work take?

In an apartment, 7 to 10 days. In a three-bedroom villa (T3), 2 to 3 weeks. The biggest factor is access to the pipes: stone or concrete walls take longer than brick.
