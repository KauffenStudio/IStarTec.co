Hiring a plumbing contractor in Portugal: licences, quotes and what to ask

Most foreign homeowners in the Algarve find their plumber through a neighbour, a Facebook group or the estate agent. Sometimes that works out well. Sometimes the job is done without a licence, without an invoice, and with a guarantee that disappears when the phone stops being answered. Portugal has a public register that tells you in a minute whether a company is licensed for building work. This guide explains how to use it, what a proper quote looks like and the questions worth asking before you sign.

Published: 13 Nov 2026 · Reading time: 8 min · Author: iStarTec

The licence: alvará and certificado

Under Law 41/2015, companies that carry out construction work in Portugal, plumbing included, need a title issued by IMPIC, the public institute that regulates the construction sector. There are two kinds:

  • Alvará: the full licence. It lists categories and subcategories of work, and a class that sets the maximum value of each job.
  • Certificado: a lighter title that only covers small jobs, up to 20% of the class 1 limit.

For a full replumb, underfloor heating across a villa or a new hot water system, the contractor should hold an alvará. A certificado is fine for a small job. A company with neither should not be doing the work, however good the price.

How to check a licence in a minute

  1. Ask the contractor for the licence number. A serious company puts it on its website, its quotes and its invoices. Ours is on the footer of every page: 90724-PAR.
  2. Search for it on the IMPIC portal, by company name, tax number (NIF) or licence number.
  3. Check that it is valid, that it covers plumbing work in buildings and that the class covers the value of your job.
  4. Check that the company on the licence is the same company that signs the quote and issues the invoice.

A PDF of a licence sent by WhatsApp is not a check. Look it up yourself.

What a proper quote looks like

A quote (orçamento) given over the phone, or written on the back of a card after a five-minute look, tells you very little. A proper quote is written after a site visit and lets you see what you are paying for:

If two quotes are very far apart, the difference is usually in what is included, not in the price of the same work. Ask each contractor to explain the gap line by line.

Gas is a separate licence

Under Decree-Law 97/2017, work on gas installations, such as disconnecting an old gas water heater (esquentador) or moving a gas point, may only be carried out by a certified gas installer. A plumber offering to "sort out the gas as well" without that certification is not allowed to do it, and it is your safety at stake.

iStarTec does not do gas work. When a job involves gas, for example replacing a gas water heater with a heat pump, a certified gas installer disconnects and caps the gas point, and we do the water and the heat pump. We explain the switch in our guide to replacing a gas water heater with a heat pump.

VAT, invoices and payments

  • VAT. As a private client, you pay VAT on the work. Ask for the total including VAT so you can compare quotes properly.
  • Invoices. Every payment should come with an invoice (fatura) issued by the company on the licence. Without one, you have no paper trail if something goes wrong.
  • Payments in stages. A payment towards equipment ordered for your job is normal. Paying the whole job upfront is not. Tie each payment to a stage of the work you can see.
  • Your NIF. If you have a Portuguese tax number, ask for it to be on the invoice.

Guarantees, in writing

There are two guarantees on a plumbing or heating job, and both should be in writing:

  • The equipment guarantee, given by the manufacturer. It usually depends on the equipment being installed and started up properly, and sometimes on registering it. Ask who registers it and keep the paperwork.
  • The workmanship guarantee, given by the contractor, for leaks and installation faults. Ask what it covers and for how long.

Ask also for photos of the pipework before walls and floors are closed. Years later, they are the best help when someone needs to drill a wall or trace a leak.

Do you need a permit?

For most plumbing and heating work inside a house, no. Article 6 of Portugal's planning and building code (RJUE, Decree-Law 555/99) exempts maintenance work and interior alterations that do not touch the structure, the façades or the roof. In an apartment, read the condominium rules first: some require notice for noisy work and set working hours.

Ten questions to ask before you sign

  1. What is your IMPIC licence number?
  2. Will your own team do the work, or a subcontractor? If a subcontractor, are they licensed too?
  3. Is the quote based on a site visit, and does it list equipment brands and models?
  4. Is VAT included, and at what rate?
  5. What is excluded, and who does it: making good, tiling, electrics, gas?
  6. When can you start, how long will it take, and will the house have water during the work?
  7. How are payments split, and will I get an invoice for each one?
  8. Who registers the equipment guarantee, and what does your own guarantee cover?
  9. Will you pressure-test the pipes before they are closed in, and can I have the photos?
  10. Who do I call if something goes wrong after the job is finished?

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a plumbing contractor in Portugal need a licence?

Yes. Under Law 41/2015, companies carrying out construction work, plumbing included, need a title from IMPIC: an alvará, or a certificado that only covers small jobs. Check it yourself on the IMPIC portal.

How do I check a company's alvará?

Search the IMPIC portal by company name, tax number (NIF) or licence number. Check that the licence is valid, covers plumbing work in buildings, has a class that covers the value of your job, and belongs to the same company that issues the quote and the invoice.

Can a plumber disconnect my gas water heater?

Only if they are also a certified gas installer. Under Decree-Law 97/2017, work on gas installations is reserved for certified gas installers. iStarTec does not do gas work: a certified installer caps the gas point and we install the water side and the heat pump.

Should I pay a deposit before the work starts?

A payment towards equipment ordered for your job is normal. Paying the whole job upfront is not. Tie each payment to a stage of the work and ask for an invoice every time you pay.

Are Portuguese quotes shown with VAT?

Not always. Many quotes show prices before VAT, written as "+ IVA". As a private client you pay VAT on the work, so ask for the total including VAT to compare quotes properly.
