How do you know you need to replumb?

There are clear signs that show up before the emergency does. If two or more of these sound familiar, it is time to plan a renovation rather than keep patching:

  • Repeated leaks — more than 2-3 leaks in the last 2 years, in different places
  • Low pressure — a weak shower even when the neighbours are not using water
  • Discoloured water — yellow or brown water after a few hours unused
  • Noisy pipes — banging, whistling or vibration when you open a tap
  • Age of the system — galvanised iron over 40 years old
  • You are renovating — use the kitchen or bathroom works to replace everything at once

What the renovation includes

When we say "replumb the whole house", we mean the complete internal network — from the incoming supply to every outlet. It does not include external connections (those belong to the local water utility) or retiling and decorating (other trades).

  • Cold water network — from the meter to every outlet
  • Hot water network — from the hot water source to every outlet
  • Main brass manifold (accessible cabinet)
  • Sub-manifolds per room (kitchen, bathrooms)
  • PEX-AL-PEX multilayer pipe (Ø16, 20, 25 mm as required)
  • Connections to existing or new taps
  • Waste networks — welded PP or German PVC (Geberit, Wavin)
  • Soil stacks and primary ventilation (in multi-storey houses)
  • Rainwater drainage and AC condensate drainage
  • Pressurisation and leak testing (10 bar over 24h)
  • Photographic record before the walls are closed
  • Full documentation and circuit plan

Multilayer, PEX, copper — what we use

PEX-AL-PEX multilayer

Our default. Combines the flexibility of PEX with the dimensional stability of aluminium. Excellent for manifold systems. Resistant to UV, corrosion and expansion.

Pure PEX

More flexible, ideal for awkward runs or long connections. Used on secondary circuits. Same 10-year warranty.

Copper

Used selectively for connections to specific equipment (boilers, cylinders) or where temperature requirements are extreme. More expensive and more labour.

What we do NOT use

Generic imported PVC for waste (inconsistent quality). PE-X from low-cost brands (they age badly). Those materials are where the next leak starts.

Why a manifold system matters

Instead of the old series layout (one pipe feeding several taps in sequence), we always install a central manifold system:

  • Every outlet has its own pipe from the manifold — a leak affects one outlet, not the whole house.
  • Even pressure — starting the washing machine does not kill the shower.
  • Easy maintenance — each circuit can be isolated individually at the manifold.
  • Clear mapping — you know exactly which tap is fed by which circuit.
  • Accessible manifold — behind a wall hatch or in a services cupboard.

How we work in an occupied home

A plumbing renovation is, alongside underfloor heating, the most invasive job we do. But there are practices that reduce the impact:

  • Phased work — we start with the main network and leave the house on water, then make the final connections in a single planned shutdown.
  • Planned water shutdown — we agree the day of the final cut with you (typically 1-3 days). A working bathroom is available before and after.
  • Minimal chasing — wherever possible we reuse the routes of the old pipework.
  • No noise out of hours — 8:30 to 17:30.
  • Dust controlled — plastic sheeting to isolate the work area, HEPA vacuum.
  • Photographic record — before the walls are closed we photograph everything. You will want those photos later.

What we do NOT do

To be clear: we do not do one-off repairs. If you have a single leak, call a local repair plumber — it is cheaper and faster. We take this work on when you are replacing the whole network, or at least an entire floor or section of the house.

We also do not retile, decorate or do finishing masonry. We make good the chases to screed level, but we do not paint or lay the final tiles — those are for the builder or decorator coordinating the renovation.

Frequently Asked Questions — Plumbing Renovation

When should I replace all the plumbing in the house?

The usual signs are: repeated leaks (more than 2-3 in recent years), low pressure at outlets, discoloured water after the supply has stood, noisy pipes, or simply age — galvanised iron networks over 40 years old should be replaced. If you are planning a large renovation (kitchen plus bathroom), it makes sense to replace all the plumbing at the same time.

How much does it cost to replumb a three-bedroom villa?

What weighs most on the price is access to the existing pipework: brick walls come in much cheaper than stone or concrete, where chasing out is heavy work. After that it is the area, the number of bathrooms, and whether the house is on one floor or several. So we quote case by case, after seeing the job: free site visit and detailed quote, answered within 24 working hours. The quote covers cold water, hot water, waste and accessible manifolds, and excludes retiling and final decoration.

Do you have to open up every wall?

Not necessarily. Wherever possible we reuse the chases the old pipework ran in, which keeps the builder's work to a minimum. In houses with stud walls (plasterboard) there is almost no destruction. In traditional stone houses there is more chasing out, but the work is planned to keep the affected area small.

Can I keep living in the house during the work?

Usually yes, though with some disruption. Water is off for 1-3 days, while we make the final connections. We plan that with you: we can fill storage tanks beforehand, or you take a couple of nights elsewhere. A working bathroom is available at all times apart from that window.

Which pipe do you use — multilayer, PEX or copper?

By default we use PEX-AL-PEX multilayer pipe for cold and hot water — it combines the flexibility of PEX with the dimensional stability of aluminium, and suits manifold systems perfectly. Copper only in specific situations (extreme temperatures, connections to particular equipment). Waste always in German-quality PP or PVC (Geberit, Wavin). No generic imported PVC.

How long does it take?

On a typical three-bedroom villa, allow 2 to 3 weeks: one week stripping out and chasing walls; one week installing the new network (pipe, manifolds and connections to every outlet); 3-4 days of pressurising, testing and making good the screed. Retiling and final decoration come afterwards, by other trades.

What warranty do you offer?

iStarTec warranty: 1 year on installation workmanship and 2 years on materials. Manufacturers add to that: multilayer and PEX pipe 10 years, brass manifolds 5 years. We always hand over an exact map of the circuits and photographs taken before the walls were closed — essential for any future work.
