Why underfloor heating

Wet underfloor heating runs PEX pipe embedded in the screed, carrying water at 35-45ºC. The heat rises slowly from the floor and warms the air evenly — no noise, no cold spots, none of the dry air of radiators or air conditioning. European studies rate it the system with the highest perceived comfort.

In the Algarve its main job is comfort in winter and the shoulder seasons (Nov-Feb), the months when houses built for summer cool down fast. The low operating temperature makes underfloor heating the most efficient option in a well-insulated house — especially paired with a heat pump.

New build vs renovation

New build

Simpler and cheaper — we build the whole base in one go, with no demolition. Best integrated at the design stage.

Partial renovation

Possible in any house. Means lifting the existing floor, and you lose about 8 cm of height. Where ceiling height is tight, a low-profile system solves it.

Apartment

Workable but needs care: minimum ceiling height, owners association approval (structural) and coordination with the neighbours below.

Older property

Quintas and traditional houses — often the ideal system. It keeps vaulted ceilings and exposed beams clear of radiators that spoil the look.

Pairing it with a heat source

Underfloor heating needs a heat source for the water — it is not a source itself. The three usual options:

  • Air source heat pump — the ideal pairing. The pump runs most efficiently at the low temperatures underfloor heating uses (COP 4-5). The lowest running cost on the market.
  • Solar thermal + heat pump — for houses that also want hot water covered. A larger investment, but an energy bill close to zero.
  • Oil or pellet boiler — the traditional option. It works, but costs a good deal more to run than a heat pump in the Algarve.

How we work in an occupied home

Underfloor heating in a renovation is the most disruptive job there is — we are not going to pretend otherwise. But there are practices that keep it manageable:

  • Phased work — we start with one room and leave the others liveable for 1-2 weeks, then move on.
  • Furniture — we help move it or recommend a removals service. Whatever stays is covered in plastic.
  • Dust controlled — plastic sheeting to isolate the work area, HEPA vacuum for the screeding.
  • No noise out of hours — 8:30 to 17:30, unless you agree otherwise.
  • No subcontracting — except the screed pour, and that is a trusted subcontractor, always the same one.

What floor finish can I use?

Almost all of them:

  • Ceramic / porcelain — the best. Conducts heat excellently and copes well with expansion.
  • Natural stone — equally good. Granite, marble, slate.
  • Floating / engineered wood — a good option; pick a product certified for underfloor heating.
  • LVT / vinyl — compatible in most ranges; check the temperature limit (~28ºC at the surface).
  • Thick solid wood — not recommended (dimensional instability).
  • Carpet — possible, but it cuts thermal output significantly.

How the installation runs

On a renovation, allow 2 to 3 weeks in total, including screed drying time.

1

Site visit

Measurements, assessment of the existing floor and structure, and setting out the heating zones. 1-2 hours.

2

Design

Room-by-room pipe layout, thermal sizing, choice of manifold and heat source. 7-10 days.

3

Demolition (if applicable)

Lifting the existing floor, levelling the base, chasing out for the feed pipes. 3-4 days.

4

Pipe installation

Insulation, PE film, Uponor PEX pipe, fixings, pressurisation. 2-3 days for a three-bedroom villa.

5

Screed + drying

Self-levelling screed over the pipe, then a minimum 7 days of drying before loading it.

6

Commissioning

Connection to the manifold and heat source, thermostat programming. A walkthrough for you, plus documentation.

Frequently Asked Questions — Underfloor Heating for Homeowners

How much does underfloor heating cost per m² in the Algarve?

The price per m² depends mostly on the state of the floor: on a slab already prepared in a new build it comes in well below a renovation that means lifting the existing floor, and a low-profile system, for cases with no height to spare, uses more expensive material. Total area counts too — the larger the continuous area, the lower the cost per m². So we quote case by case: free site visit and detailed quote, answered within 24 working hours. The quote covers insulation, Uponor PEX pipe, manifolds, conduit and labour, and excludes the final floor finish and the heat source.

Can I put underfloor heating in an existing house?

Yes, it is one of the most requested renovation jobs in the Algarve. It means lifting the existing floor, laying insulation and pipe, screeding over the top and relaying the floor. You lose about 8 cm of height (5-6 cm with some thinner systems). Where height is tight, low-profile systems are more expensive but workable.

Does it work well with a heat pump?

Yes — it is in fact the ideal pairing. Underfloor heating runs at low temperature (35-45ºC) and a heat pump is at its most efficient in exactly that range. Heat pump plus underfloor heating gives the lowest running cost on the market for a house in the Algarve.

How long does it take to install?

On a renovation, allow 2 to 3 weeks: 3-4 days to lift the floor and prepare the base; 2-3 days to lay insulation and pipe; 1 day of screeding; 5-7 days for the screed to dry; then the final floor finish (which may be another contractor). A new build is faster — about a week for pipe and screed.

What floor finish can I use?

Almost all of them work — ceramic and porcelain are the best (they conduct heat very well), natural stone too. Floating engineered wood and LVT vinyl are also compatible provided they are certified for underfloor heating. Thick solid wood is not recommended.

Is it worth it in the Algarve, where it is not that cold?

It is worth it if you want real comfort in winter and the shoulder seasons. Algarve houses are built for summer and cool down fast — underfloor heating removes the cold-floor feeling through the damp months (Nov-Feb). Paired with a heat pump, it is the cheapest heating system to run in a well-insulated house.
